# Editing rules (the agent follows these for every reel)

Derived from the two reference reels analysed in the Reel Kit teardown. Tune them with pilot results.

## Hook (first 1.5 s)
- The key visual AND the headline are on screen within 1.5 s. Speech starts within 1.0 s.
- Open with the object, the number or the promise, never with a logo or "Hi, I'm...".
- Hook patterns that work: a bold claim ("This one dashboard replaces five reports"),
  a number ("We cut response time by 62%"), a myth ("You might think AI agents sound robotic").

## Structure
- Use a numbered structure the viewer can follow: First / Second / Third, or Step 1-4.
- Every claim gets a visual proof: a counter, a checklist ticking, a UI card, a before/after.
- One CTA, one keyword, at the very end, on a background colour change.

## Pacing
- Something visibly changes at least every 0.6-1.0 s (a word, a card, a tick, a zoom).
- No static stretch longer than 1.5 s. Each beat lasts 0.6-6 s.
- Speech pace 2.5-4.5 words per second (about 150-270 wpm); the reference reels ran ~170-205 wpm.

## Captions
- Word-by-word reveal in the middle third of the screen, as the headline, not tiny subtitles.
- Upcoming words shown faint, spoken words solid.
- Exactly one emphasis word per line: italic serif in the accent colour.
- Keep text inside the safe area: 80 px from left, 140 px from right (platform icons),
  220 px from top, 420 px from bottom (platform captions and buttons).

## Look
- Three-colour system from brand/brand.json. The accent colour is only for emphasis and the CTA.
- Background flips mark acts: light = explain, dark = proof/myths, accent = CTA.
- A small chapter label at the top-left and a progress counter top-right (e.g. STEP 2/4).

## Audio
- One clear voice. Integrated loudness about -14 LUFS, true peak at or below -1 dBTP.
- Music sits about 18 dB under the voice. Small UI sound effects on reveals and ticks.

## Facts and rights
- Every number, name or claim must come from the brief's fact sheet in facts/. The build fails otherwise.
- Use only footage, music, fonts and images we own or have a licence for. Reference reels are
  studied for structure and timing only - never reuse their footage, music or artwork.
